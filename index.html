03 / open when

Five letters, for five versions of you

Starter letters, written for the long-distance part of us. Edit them until every line sounds unmistakably like you.

Esha, I hate that I can't just show up and sit next to you right now. I hate that ordinary little moments are happening back home that I only get to hear about later, and that sometimes I want to tell you something and remember there is an entire ocean and a very rude time difference between us. But you are not becoming a smaller part of my life just because you're not physically in it every day. You are still in the things I want to tell someone first. You're in the stories I save because I know exactly how you'd react. You're in the version of home I carry around with me. I miss you in the boring moments most. And I love you in all of them. Love you. Across all the miles, always.
My Esha, Whoever is annoying you: I have a strongly worded imaginary email ready. But also, you do not have to be impressive or productive or entertaining every second of every day. You can have a bad day without turning it into a personality flaw. You can call me with a perfectly organised explanation, a completely incoherent rant, or just silence. You don't have to make your feelings sound neat for me. You don't have to earn the right to be cared about by being okay. I am on your team on the days you feel unstoppable and on the days you feel like a phone on 2% battery. Especially those days. Now drink some water. This is an order from a very far away but extremely bossy friend.
Hi, favourite archive of my nonsense, A friendship becomes a whole language. A look, a word, a reference nobody else understands — and suddenly you're both back in a moment that happened ages ago. We have collected so many tiny things. Not all of them would sound important if I listed them to someone else. That's the point. They are ours. We made a whole world out of ordinary days, accidental jokes, conversations that went on too long, and moments I didn't know I would miss until I was far away. No distance gets to retroactively make those moments less real. They happened. They changed me. You're part of my life in a way that doesn't disappear when the scenery changes.
Esha, I can't promise that every week will look the same. There will be busy days, missed calls, messages answered late, and things happening in two different lives at once. I don't want us to mistake every quiet patch for a catastrophe. I want us to keep choosing each other in the ways we can: random updates, voice notes that should have been three sentences, remembering the little details, and saying “hey, I miss you” instead of pretending we're too cool to care. I don't need our friendship to stay frozen exactly as it was. I want it to grow with us. I want future versions of us to have new jokes, new stories, new places to meet — and still recognise each other immediately. We're allowed to change. I'm still rooting for us.
My Esha, I don't think I will ever find a sentence big enough for what it means to have you in my life, so I am going to try with the small truths instead. You are one of the people who make my life feel like mine. You belong to so many chapters of my story that I cannot tell it honestly without you appearing somewhere in it. And now I am starting a chapter in a different country, building a life I have wanted for so long, and somehow one of the things I wish I could pack in my suitcase is the ability to be near you whenever I want. I am excited about the life I'm making here. That doesn't mean I love the life and the people I left behind any less. Both things can be true. I can be grateful for this new beginning and still wish I could teleport home for one stupid little conversation. If you ever wonder whether you still matter from so far away, come back to this page. You matter on ordinary days, dramatic days, days we talk for hours, and days we only send one meme. You matter when I'm happy here. You matter when I miss home. You matter without having to prove anything. There is an Ahmedabad-shaped part of my heart, and you are woven into it. There is also a new little life growing here in Illinois, and I want to keep bringing you into it — showing you things, telling you stories, making plans for when our maps finally overlap again. Until then, this tiny website is me trying to put my arms around a distance I can't cross today. I love you. Thank you for being my person. Always, me ♡

07 / the last page

One last thing.

If home can be a person, distance has some explaining to do.

I don't know exactly what the next few years will look like. I know there will be things I wish I could tell you in person, things we'll have to catch up on, and moments when I wish the world were a little smaller.

My life getting bigger doesn't mean the people I love get smaller. There is room for this new life and for you. There always will be.

Save me a place in your next story. I'll keep saving you a place in mine.
